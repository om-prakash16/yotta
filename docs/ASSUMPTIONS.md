# Assumptions and trade-offs

1. **One school year, AM runs only.** One route per bus; each active stop can appear on multiple routes. `route_stops` is the N:M bridge between Route and Stop.
2. **Synthetic geography.** Zones have synthetic planar centroids. Student and stop coordinates are generated around their home zone with 0.5 km standard deviation. Road distance is 1.3 times straight-line segment distance; this is a toy-city approximation, not real road routing.
3. **Equitable stop placement.** Stops are placed with a 50/50 blend of zone demand weights and an even allocation across approved zones. This is intended to avoid starving lower-density/rural service areas while retaining demand awareness.
4. **Accessibility assumptions.** 96% of generated stops are wheelchair-accessible. Vehicle accessibility is more likely on mini buses (90%), and also represented on standard (75%) and large (75%) buses; the vehicle carries 2 or 4 wheelchair positions based on vehicle type. This is an accessibility-forward synthetic fleet assumption, not a claim about a real fleet.
5. **IDs.** Required ID formats are used verbatim; supporting tables follow the same style (`ZONE-`, `DRV-`, `GRD-`, `DIS-2026-`, `CAP-2026-`, `AEV-2026-`, `ATT-2026-0000001`). `ASN-<student-id>-2026` makes Assignment 1:1 with Student for the year, so reassignment history is carried by `assignment_events`, and day-level history by `attendance_events` (route/stop snapshot).
6. **Capacity counts everyone assigned during the school year.** The baseline checks assigned riders against the minimum bus capacity in its history. This is conservative and avoids daily bin-packing; it may understate available capacity after a student withdraws. Capacity changes in the valid baseline never fall below this conservative rider count; the injected anomaly is the deliberate exception.
7. **Pickup windows and walking limits.** Each student's pickup window is 75 minutes. Grade K–5 walking distance is capped at 0.8 km; grades 6–12 at 1.2 km. Route starts are generated at 06:35, 06:40 or 06:45 so their stop times are biased toward the overlapping grade windows.
8. **Assignment scoring.** Hard constraints are evaluated before scoring. Wheelchair-required students are considered first. The deterministic score is `straight_line_walk_km + 1.5 × route_load_ratio`; the load penalty helps balance route occupancy without overriding safety constraints. It is a heuristic, not a globally optimal solver.
9. **Notifications.** Only the primary guardian is contacted. Messages contain student ID, stop ID/name and date, never a home address. Phones use the fictional 555-0100–555-0199 block; emails use reserved `example.org` addresses.
10. **Unapproved address exposure.** This privacy anomaly is modelled as a notification that discloses a student's synthetic home address. Unapproved *stops* are a separate concept (`stop_status=proposed`) and are kept off routes.
11. **Impossible travel.** The validator flags implied route-segment speeds above 80 km/h. Baseline segment speeds are generated between 20 and 45 km/h.
12. **Anomalies are single-purpose.** Candidate records are pre-checked so each injection triggers its intended rule. Assignment-changing anomalies are injected before capacity overflow so later mutations cannot neutralise the capacity case. Counts are parameterised (5 per type full / 3 starter / 2 test), subject to valid candidates existing.
13. **Attendance precedence.** Absence takes precedence over route cancellation, which takes precedence over missed pickup, which takes precedence over present. A disruption identifier is retained on the attendance event even when an absence takes precedence.
14. **Lifecycle and history.** Attendance carries route/stop snapshots; event history is append-only. A subsequent assignment change does not rewrite a historical attendance record.
15. **No external data.** The dataset is fully synthetic. There are no external source URLs, retrieval dates or redistribution licences to record. Real personal records and live contact endpoints are not used.

## Full-profile baseline observations

With seed `20261022`, the clean 5,000-student profile assigns 4,781 students (95.62%). Wheelchair-required coverage is 96.15% versus 95.61% for students without that requirement; rural coverage is 86.03%, suburban 97.70% and urban 99.39%. The subgroup rates are emitted in `metrics.json`. Rural gaps and unassigned exceptions remain explicit optimisation targets; they are not concealed by the overall assignment rate.
